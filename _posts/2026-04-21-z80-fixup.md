The last sprint (5.0) was a survey to discover how good our 
z80 opcode coverage, and the results were not so great, with 
only 86% opcode coverage. So we created a new urgent story story
to fix the omissions (5.0.5) and scheduled it for immediate 
execution. 

Implementation took a while, as there are a couple of large 
classes of `LD,` instructions that needed to be added to our 
already turgid `LD,` handler. And we needed some new syntax
words too to deal with indirect loads from immediate addresses.

Code review finds some medium and low issues, nothing too serious.

We'll have a look at the changes and have the scrum master run the 
coverage report at the end. 

## assembler.asm

FOURTEEN missing opcodes were covered with this simple pattern (just 
a subset show here):

![14 Missing opcodes](/antforth/assets/images/2026-04-21/simple_missing.png)


`ADC` and `SBC` are added and look very similar, here's `ADC`:


![ADC implementation](/antforth/assets/images/2026-04-21/adc.png)

My personal fave `DJNZ` finally joins the party:

![DJNZ implementation](/antforth/assets/images/2026-04-21/djnz.png)

The implementation is trickier than you might expect, as it can handle 
both immediate addresses and labels, and has to deal with the undefined 
label case.

`RST` is nice and simple:

![RST implementation](/antforth/assets/images/2026-04-21/rst.png)

It takes a bare integer and doesn't require the `#` immediate constant 
tagging word, because `RST` only has that one form so there's nothing 
to disambiguate.


`RLD` and `RRD` are tiny:

![RLD/RRD implementation](/antforth/assets/images/2026-04-21/rld_rrd.png)

Here's the new `()` syntax word to denote absolute memory addresses:

![Absolute address word](/antforth/assets/images/2026-04-21/absolute_address.png)

And we needed new words for `(BC)` and `(DE)` - fortunately quite simple:

![(BC) and (DE) words](/antforth/assets/images/2026-04-21/paren_bc_paren_de.png)

One final wrinkle: we need words to represent the registers `I` and `R`, 
but we can't use `I` for this, because it's already a Forth word meaning 
"the current loop index". And it's used a lot for that purpose, whereas 
hardly anyone ever uses the I or R registers. Therefore we've taken the
decision to name their words `IREG` and `RREG`:

![I and R registerwords](/antforth/assets/images/2026-04-21/ireg_rreg.png)
