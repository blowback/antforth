## Challenges

### 1. Story 10.10 hardware-smoke instructions were under-contextualised

Project-lead feedback: *"human UAT was a bit frustrating in the last story, as there wasn't enough context about what was being asked, and some of the language was baffling."*

Story 10.10's hardware-smoke section asked for 12 line-by-line copy-paste runs without restating *what each line was actually checking*. The reader had to flip back to the AC table to know whether trailing-space mattered, whether `( hi lo true )` order was the bug or the feature, etc. The verdict-cell prose ("PASS-with-waiver", "algorithmic-class reading") leaked into the instruction text where plain "do this, expect this" rows were called for.

This is **Epic 9 retro Action #5 (`feedback_plain_qa_language.md`) recurring on Epic 10 in a new place.** We applied plain prose to verdict tables but not to human-UAT instruction blocks. Captured as Action Item #4 below.

### 2. AC-text drafting errata caught at implementation time across most stories

Six of nine implementation stories caught AC errata at dev time:

- **10.4 AC #2** — boundary-value arithmetic claimed `0 $8000 0 1 D-` → `($FFFF $7FFF)`; correct `(0 $7FFF)`.
- **10.6** — multiple AC test rows: `-2 -1 -1 UM/MOD` expected-value drift; `0 -10 3 SM/REM` stack-order confusion (`0 -10` interpreted as -10 instead of unsigned 65526).
- **10.7 AC #15** — sanity test `123 0 <# #S #>` → 8060928 not 123 (E10-D1 stack-order assumption violated).
- **10.8 AC #15** — `HEX 8000 0 D. DECIMAL` BASE-switch semantics ambiguity.
- **10.9 AC #14a** — div-by-zero wording mismatched silent baseline; `/COUNTED-STRING` length stated as 16, actual 15; `MAX-D` `.S` shape ambiguous.
- **10.10 AC #5** — 4 spec-text drafting errors (E10-D1 input-order reversed in 2 rows; trailing-space wrong in 2 rows; BASE-switch confusion in 1 row).

Epic 9 retro Action #6 added an AC-trace-check for `.` / `U.` / `.R` / `U.R` / `D.` / `D.R`. It improved coverage but didn't extend to **double-cell stack-order** or **pictured-output input ordering**. Action Item #5 below extends the trace-check to E10-D1 ECCs.

### 3. §-number citation drift across upstream artefacts

Caught and back-ported during Epic 10:

- `2@` cited as §6.1.0290 in `architecture.md` and `epics.md` — correct §6.1.0350 (Story 10.2 finding).
- `M+` typo §8.6.1.1830 in `epics.md:508` — correct §8.6.1.1830 (Story 10.4 finding).
- `HOLDS` cited as §6.2.1625 in epic spec — correct §6.2.1675 (Story 10.7 finding).
- `.R` cited as §6.1.0310 in epic spec — §6.1.0310 is `2!`; correct §6.2.0210 (Story 10.8 finding).
- `HOLDS` form `Forth 2012/2014` retagged canonical `Forth 2014` (Story 10.10 in-place fix).

Pattern vindicates `feedback_systematic_reference_check.md` — every implementation story caught at least one §-number drift between epic-spec text and DPANS94 ground truth.

### 4. NFR5 short-primitive literal-reading shortfall

Analytic T-state counting found `D+` 378% / `D-` 436% / `M*` 185% / `UM/MOD` 70% over the ≤20% gate on the **literal reading.** Algorithmic-class reading (excluding 156 T constant threading overhead per call) gives `D+` 66% / `D-` 124% / `UM*` kernel 13% / `UM/MOD` ~49% — closer to gate but still over.

Project-lead waiver granted 2026-04-25 on the algorithmic-class reading; literal-reading shortfall carried into Epic 12 as known-issue. Same shape as Epic 9's NFR1 PASS-with-finding pattern — a literal-percentage gate doesn't fit primitives whose absolute T-state count is dominated by a fixed threading overhead. Action Item #6 below adopts the algorithmic-class form from day one for Epic 11.

### 5. Size-prediction band bias on structural-change stories

Tight on additive DEFCODE (10.2 / 10.4 / 10.5 / 10.9 within band). Off on stories with structural side-effects:

- 10.3 +47% over (inline 32-bit accumulator)
- 10.6 +37% over (`(?3)` helper opcode + PICK overhead)
- 10.7 over band (42-byte BSS UserArea growth outside the estimate)
- 10.8 *negative* (dedup of hand-rolled paths)

Same calibration observation as Epic 9 (which flagged duplication-then-consolidation). Predictions are bytecount-of-new-DEFCODE estimates, not structure-aware. Carried forward as Action Item #3 with explicit upside budget for structural-change stories.

### 6. Self-review zero-finding states remained suspect

Stories 10.3, 10.7, 10.8, and 10.10 all had self-review reports that found nothing material; external/code-review pass found 1 HIGH (10.3 — silent data corruption), or multiple MED/LOW (10.7/10.8/10.10). Story 10.10 in particular had a self-rationalised NFR5 PASS verdict that the senior-dev review caught and downgraded to PASS-with-finding pending project-lead waiver.

This is `feedback_adversarial_review.md` continuing to hold — **zero-finding self-reviews are a smell, not a victory.** Always run the second LLM pass.

## Key Insights

1. **The 100% milestone is unglamorous by design.** Project-lead summary: *"work was mechanistic, unglamorous and a slog at times but we got there."* The headline is one row in `compliance.md`, but the path was nine straight DEFCODE/DEFWORD stories grinding through §6.1. **Anyone can ship 90%; the slog is what makes the absence of asterisks real.** This shape is itself a Phase-2 lesson — the closer-to-completeness epics are not innovation-driven, they are completion-driven.

2. **Party-mode is the right unblock for AC ambiguity that would otherwise become a permanent footnote.** ENVIRONMENT? was on track to be a forever "deliberate omission." A 10-agent party-mode discussion (2026-04-20) reclassified it to a Story 10.9 deliverable. **One sprint-change conversation eliminated the asterisk on the 100% claim for the entire product lifetime.** Disproportionate ROI on facilitation time.

3. **DEFWORD-on-primitives is a structural ROM-shrinking lever.** Story 10.8's net −7-byte rewrite of 6 Core number-print words shows that consolidation-after-foundation reliably shrinks the kernel even while adding capability. Same pattern as Epic 9.4's sign-in-body refactor (−31 B). When ROM pressure surfaces in Epic 12/13, these consolidation moves are real budget.

4. **Threading overhead dominates short-primitive benchmarks; literal-percentage gates don't fit the work.** ~156 T constant per DEFCODE call swamps a 200-T algorithm. The algorithmic-class reading is the meaningful one for Epic 11+ NFR-equivalent gates. Don't write a literal-percentage gate that short primitives can't physically meet under threading overhead.

5. **Audit-only stories (10.1, 10.10) tempt zero-finding self-reviews and need adversarial discipline most.** Both Epic-10 audit stories had self-reviews that missed real findings (10.1 missed the FR15/NFR10 self-authorisation; 10.10 missed the self-rationalised NFR5 verdict cell). External review found them. **Audit-only does not mean review-light** — if anything the opposite, because audit work is what the audit-class adversarial review is designed to test.

6. **AC-trace-check (Epic 9 #6) is necessary but not sufficient.** Catching `.` vs `U.` signed-print errors alone left double-cell stack-order ECCs (E10-D1 low-on-TOS) and pictured-output input ordering uncovered. Epic 11 will face an analogous risk on THROW-code semantics — extending the trace-check to ECC-level invariants is Action Item #5.

7. **Hardware smoke remains the gold standard, but the smoke instructions need plain-prose discipline too.** 12/12 PASS on real MicroBeast was definitive — but the dev-side UAT friction from baffling instruction text suggests that "plain QA prose" (Epic 9 retro Action #5) needs to extend from verdict cells into the smoke ask itself. Captured as Action Item #4.
