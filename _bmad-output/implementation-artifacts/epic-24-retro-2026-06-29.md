## What went well

1. **The lockless `TICKS` seqlock landed correct first time.** A tear-free
   4-byte read of a counter mutated by the ISR, sidestepping the `LD A,I` P/V
   erratum by avoiding DI/EI entirely (read high → low → re-read high, retry on
   mismatch). Genuinely tricky concurrency primitive; the code review confirmed
   it sound. The carry-into-high-word handling (16-bit `INC` sets no flags →
   explicit `LD A,H / OR L` zero test) was also caught and got right at spec
   time.

2. **Designed for Epic 25, not just for now.** The monotonic up-counter (vs
   TRAFFIC's down-counter) lets `DELAY` ride a stack-held target with no shared
   mutable cell, so per-task delays fall out for free (FR15). The `(DELAY)` wait
   loop is factored to exactly one site so 25.3's yielding rewrite drops in a
   single `PAUSE` with no change to the target math. Architecture decisions made
   with the next epic already in hand.

3. **Load-bearing CR held.** The close-out review found the cross-bank
   `tick_count` placement invariant (ISR + `TICKS` read it whichever bank is
   mapped, so it MUST stay below `$8000`) had no build guard. Fixed in-session
   with a one-line `ASSERT $ <= SLOT2_WINDOW_BASE`. CR findings 1 (Ctrl-C
   dangling ISR) and 2 (unescapable busy-wait) were correctly triaged to the
   future keyboard-break-out story rather than gold-plated now.

4. **S9 silicon cadence held end-to-end.** iz-cpm-banking does not model the
   0xFDC7 user interrupt, so HW-smoke was non-negotiable for every timed claim —
   and was delivered for both stories. Zero deferred-smoke debt at epic close.

5. **The double-producer `PUSH BC` gotcha did not repeat.** The 24.1 spec
   finding (a `( -- d )` producer must `PUSH BC`, not copy the `2@` shape, which
   consumes its address arg) was applied to `TICKS` and carried cleanly into
   `DELAY`/`MS`. `project_double_producer_push_bc` paid off.

## What didn't — challenges & growth areas

1. **The `$8000` straddle halt recurred a 4th time** (24.2's `minus-bank-ldir`
   probe), fixed reactively again — *despite* Story 23.8 having "closed"
   AI-22-5 / AI-23-1. The 23.8 migration only hardened probes at-risk at 23.8's
   kernel size; the next +97 B pushed a *fresh* colon-body probe across the
   window. The whack-a-mole is not retired — it was deferred per-probe. Now a
   four-epic pattern. **This is the central finding, and it has a definitive
   disposition this time (AI-24-1).**

2. **Verdict-grep harness papercut recurred** — `test-repl-timer` re-derived the
   column-0 anchor + `grep -a` + `\r`-strip hygiene from scratch. AI-23-4's 4th+
   recurrence. The "weigh against ceremony" caveat has now been overtaken by
   recurrence cost (AI-24-2).

3. **Emulator concurrency coverage is structurally thin (NFR-P6-14).** Both
   stories carry an emulator-structural gate + deferred HW-smoke for all timed
   behaviour, plus the "nonzero wait busy-waits forever under emulation" trap
   (flagged as the #1 dev trap). Not a defect — a platform reality — but Epic
   25's REPL-concurrency / `KEY`-yield work will widen this gap, and more of the
   real behaviour will only ever be provable on silicon.

## Key takeaways

1. **Get the concurrency primitive right at spec time and CR confirms it** — the
   lockless `TICKS` read, the carry trap, and the erratum-avoidance were all
   reasoned through before code and held up under review. Cheaper than finding
   tearing on silicon.
2. **Build the foundation for the epic after next, not just the current one** —
   the up-counter + `(DELAY)` seam mean Epic 25's yielding `DELAY` is a
   one-`PAUSE` edit, and per-task delays need no new mechanism.
3. **A fragility deferred per-instance recurs per-instance** — Story 23.8
   migrated at-risk probes but built no standing guard, so the straddle returned
   on the very next kernel-growth story. A predicted, repeatedly-paid fragility
   finally gets a structural fix (AI-24-1) instead of a fifth reactive patch.
4. **Recurrence is the signal that flips a "weigh against ceremony" call** —
   AI-23-4 was optional three times; at the fourth re-derivation the helper is
   cheaper than the boilerplate (AI-24-2).
