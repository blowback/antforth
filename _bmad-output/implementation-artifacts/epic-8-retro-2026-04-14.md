## Successes

- **Target shattered on first story.** Story 8.1 alone delivered 44 bytes — the entire epic's optimistic target — because CHAR's restructure unlocked a structural simplification nobody pre-sized: tib_in can be computed at exit as `parse_ptr - tib_addr` via a single `SBC HL,DE` instead of tracked incrementally, which eliminated two copies of a 17-byte PUSH/LD/INC/LD/POP dance inside the inner parse loops. The `.char_result` scratch byte went away as a bonus. Epic 7.2's "second-order savings" lesson continues to compound in exactly the predicted direction.
- **Dead-code removal reframed as an EXX story.** `(ABORT")`'s `CALL rpush_de` + `INC IX / INC IX` unwind were both pure no-ops — ABORT resets SP/IX wholesale and the saved IP was never read back. Seven bytes deleted, zero replacement mechanism needed. The lesson: audits of save/restore pairs should ask "is anything actually reading the saved value?" before reaching for EXX.
- **Formatting pipeline Path 1 proven, then applied at scale.** Story 8.2 landed DOT + U. as a mechanical pair exactly to spec (−4 bytes, 0% variance). That de-risked Story 8.3, which took the same pattern into the structurally harder `.R` and `.S` and delivered 27 bytes — beating the optimistic target on a Medium-risk story. Two-story ramp from trivial to complex is a reusable approach.
- **Estimation variance collapsed on mechanical stories.** Epic 7: max variance +167%. Epic 8: 8.2 landed exact; 8.3 landed within optimistic range (+4% over); only 8.1 ran away — and that was *upside from a structural breakthrough*, not miscounted opcodes. The "count real Z80 opcodes, not assumed" discipline from Epic 6 is now second nature.
- **Shadow BC' as preservation slot generalised to `.S`.** Story 8.3's `.S` used BC' to hold the original TOS instead of caching it via `rpush_bc` to IX. The `.dots_print_tos` internal path uses an advanced double-EXX round-trip (`EXX / PUSH BC / EXX / POP BC`) to bring the preserved TOS into main BC — the first documented instance of the pattern. Story 8.4 folds this into the convention doc with a "use only when plain EXX-swap is insufficient" hedge.
- **Epic 7 retro action item #5 delivered.** `docs/register-conventions.md` now exists as the single authoritative source for the four hard rules, Group A/B patterns, leaf-level rule, A-survives-EXX idiom, shadow BC' preservation slot, complete EXX-using word list, and out-of-scope deferrals. `src/inner_interpreter.asm` carries a two-line pointer comment so source-first readers find it.
- **Code reviews continue to earn their keep — even on docs.** Story 8.4's review surfaced 1 HIGH (inaccurate AF' coverage claim), 2 MEDIUM, and 5 LOW findings on what looked like "just a markdown file." Adversarial review remains load-bearing; absence of findings would be suspect.
- **Zero regressions, zero new tech debt, zero production incidents** across all 4 stories. Manually verified on MicroBeast hardware per Epic 6/7 convention.

## Challenges

### 1. Story 8.1 Overshoot Hides a Sizing Signal

Estimate 10–14 bytes, actual 44 bytes. +214% upside. This is the fourth consecutive epic where a register-liberation story has overshot by a large factor (Epic 6's 6.3 at +303%, Epic 7's 7.2 at +167%, now Epic 8's 8.1 at +214%). The pattern is consistent: **first-order save/restore savings are small; the real wins come from structural simplifications the freed register set makes *possible but not obvious*.**

The story specs repeatedly size only the direct substitution and flag the second-order as "may be eliminable." By now that framing systematically understates. Consider: *future register-liberation stories should explicitly budget a "structural simplification" allowance — maybe +100% over first-order estimate — and mark it upside rather than pretending it's out-of-scope.*

**Not quite an action item yet** — the overshoot is consistently *upside* and project-lead comfort with schedule slop is high. But the sizing model is wrong and we should acknowledge that.

### 2. Diminishing-Returns Signal Is Clear

Story 8.2 (the easy mechanical one) saved exactly 4 bytes, on target, no upside. Story 8.3 retained all six of `.R`/`.S`'s memory scratch slots because the BDOS-call pressure inside the pad-emit and `.dots_walk` loops structurally blocks register-resident elimination — `bdos_putchar` clobbers BC/DE/HL/A on every iteration, so shadow preservation gives nothing across the call. 8.3 saved 27 bytes on entry/exit/IX-cache replacement, but the `.dotr_*` and `.dots_*` data scratch is load-bearing and stays.

This confirms the Epic 8 planning framing ("diminishing returns expected and accepted — closes out the shadow-register vein rather than opening new ground"). Further EXX work in the formatting pipeline would require a BDOS-call contract change (e.g., a register-preserving `bdos_putchar` wrapper that restores BC/HL around the call), which is an architectural change disproportionate to the remaining marginal yield.

### 3. Category D Exclusions Remain Untouched by Design

The Epic 7 retro flagged `(DO)` runtime, `(;CODE)`/DOES> exit, INTERPRET loop, and a few others as deferred/out-of-scope. Epic 8 did not revisit them. Story 8.4's convention doc §8 formally documents them as *deliberately deferred* (INTERPRET is the main thread; `(DO)` and `(;CODE)` have functional rpush semantics tied to loop state and DOES> behaviour). These should be treated as "not candidates" rather than "candidates we haven't gotten to" — the distinction matters for future planning.

### 4. AF' / `EX AF,AF'` Remains Unused

Epic 7 flagged this as a dormant resource. Epic 8's survey (and Story 8.4's §8) concluded the same: survey showed byte-neutral in simple PUSH AF/POP AF pairs, marginal yield in scratch-variable migrations (0–10 bytes for high analysis cost). No consumer has emerged. Carry forward as documented-but-not-pursued; flag if a future story surfaces a genuine need.

## Key Insights

1. **Register liberation consistently unlocks upside the story spec cannot pre-size.** Epic 6's 6.3 (+303%), Epic 7's 7.2 (+167%), Epic 8's 8.1 (+214%) — three consecutive big overshoots, all upside, all from *structural simplifications the freed registers enabled*, not from miscounted opcodes. The first-order estimate (count save/restore → EXX bytes) is reliable; the total estimate is not. Plan for the delta explicitly.

2. **"Is anything reading this saved value?" is a pre-EXX question.** Story 8.1's `(ABORT")` transformation wasn't an EXX conversion — it was recognising that 7 bytes of rpush + unwind accomplished nothing because the saved DE was never consumed and ABORT resets stacks wholesale anyway. Future save/restore audits should ask this before assuming EXX is the answer.

3. **BDOS-call pressure is the floor on register-resident elimination.** `.R`/`.S`'s data scratch couldn't be removed because `bdos_putchar` clobbers BC/DE/HL/A every loop iteration. Shadow preservation doesn't help across a BDOS call. This generalises: *any refactor seeking to eliminate memory scratch inside a BDOS-calling loop will fail at this wall unless the BDOS interface is itself changed.*

4. **The "two-story ramp" is a reusable pattern for staged refactors.** Story 8.2 (trivial mechanical — DOT/U.) proved Path 1 on the formatting pipeline in 4 bytes. Story 8.3 (structurally hard — .R/.S) then took the same pattern into difficult territory with confidence. Risk is de-risked by the intermediate; scope is split so either story is tractable on its own. Consider explicitly for future multi-word refactor epics.

5. **Documentation stories deserve adversarial review too.** Story 8.4 looked like a markdown write-up; review surfaced 8 findings including 1 HIGH (factual error about AF'). Docs that future agents will read as authoritative are load-bearing. Review them like code.

6. **Shadow BC' as TOS-preservation slot generalises beyond `( x -- ... )` words.** Epic 7.3 established this for NUMBER? (parse failure → restore x). Epic 8.3 used it for `.S` (`( -- )` word, but needs original TOS as part of display). Same pattern, different motivation. The doc (§6) now frames it as "the original TOS is sitting in BC' for free after a plain entry EXX — use it when you need the original value on any path."
