## Successes

- **Survey-then-fix pattern** — The standout process innovation of this epic. Story 5.0 audited Z80 coverage and found exactly 27 gaps, enabling Story 5.0.5 to close them with precise, enumerated acceptance criteria. Story 5.3 audited ANS compliance and found 72.2%, enabling Story 5.4 to pick the 15 quickest wins. Measure first, act on data. Produces well-scoped implementation stories with zero ambiguity.
- **Epic 4's tag encoding investment compounded** — The unified 0xFF class/index encoding from Story 4.3.5 made adding new tag indices in Story 5.0.5 trivial. (BC), (DE), (), IREG, RREG all slotted into existing classes without architectural changes. Architectural investments compound across epics.
- **Mid-epic scope growth handled cleanly** — Stories 5.0.5 and 5.4 were unplanned additions that emerged naturally from survey findings. Both were well-scoped because the surveys had already done the discovery work. Epic grew from 4 to 6 stories without disruption.
- **Review safety net continues to catch register bugs** — BC register preservation bugs caught in Story 5.1 (`(` word) and Story 5.2 (DOMARKER LDIR zeroing BC). Code reviews remain the primary defence against Z80 register-contract violations.
- **REPL test discipline held** — 44 new tests, all piped Forth scripts, zero assembly thread extensions. Test count grew from 214 to 258. Every implementation story shipped with comprehensive test coverage.
- **100% Z80 coverage and 83.5% ANS compliance are real milestones** — The system is now verifiably complete for Z80 assembly and approaching ANS standards compliance.
- **Zero regressions, zero new tech debt, zero production incidents** across all 6 stories.

## Challenges

### 1. Register-Contract Violations (Still the Dominant Bug Class)

Appeared in at least 3 of 4 implementation stories (5.0.5, 5.1, 5.2):
- Story 5.1: `(` word initially forgot to save/restore BC (TOS) around the scan loop
- Story 5.2: DOMARKER initially forgot that LDIR zeroes BC, destroying TOS
- Story 5.0.5: `asm_emit_byte` clobbering HL affected absolute-address LD forms

**Resolution:** Accepted as inherent to Z80 assembly development. Save-and-restore-everything is not performant. The review process catches every instance. The team agreed this is the cost of doing business on the Z80, and the review safety net is working.

### 2. Story Spec Errors (Normal Iteration)

Appeared in 3 of 4 implementation stories:
- Story 5.1: Test expected values wrong (5 + 3 = 8, not 13)
- Story 5.4: >BODY offset wrong (xt+5, not xt+3 — CREATE has 2-byte does-addr slot)
- Story 5.4: ABORT" test had inverted 0= logic

**Resolution:** Accepted as normal iteration. Specs are written before implementation; some details can only be verified during coding. The important thing is the dev agent verifies claims against actual source code rather than blindly following specs.

### 3. Story 5.3 Status Discrepancy

Story file shows `Status: ready-for-dev` but sprint-status.yaml shows `done`. Minor cosmetic issue.

## Key Insights

1. **Survey-then-fix is a reusable pattern** — When working toward "completeness" of anything, audit first, then implement from findings. Produces precise, well-scoped stories. Should be the standard approach for future completeness work.
2. **Architectural investments compound across epics** — Epic 4's tag encoding, REPL test infrastructure, and hash-based dictionary all made Epic 5 cheaper to build. Worth doing refactors when the time is right.
3. **Register-contract bugs are Z80's nature** — Accept them, keep reviews adversarial. Don't over-engineer save/restore at the cost of performance.
4. **Epic scope growth can be healthy** — When driven by survey findings (not scope creep), mid-epic additions add real value without disruption.
5. **MVP is complete** — Five epics took AntForth from nothing to a boot-disk-ready Forth system with built-in Z80 assembler, 83.5% ANS compliance, and 258 automated tests.
