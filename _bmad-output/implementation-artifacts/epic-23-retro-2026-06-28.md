## What went well

1. **CR stayed load-bearing — and it paid off.** The dry-pass relaxation Epic 22
   warned against (AI-22-4) was correctly *not* taken. Full-intensity review on
   23.2/23.3 surfaced a **silent correctness defect**: a banked colon body growing
   past `$C000` would have its doer read through the wrong bank window. That finding
   became the entire Story 23.6, plus a critical `ALLOT` 16-bit-wrap bug caught in
   23.6's own review. Without load-bearing CR both ship silently.

2. **Standards honesty over standards theater.** 23.4 declined to rubber-stamp the
   spec: `DOUBLE`/`DOUBLE-EXT`/`SEARCH-ORDER-EXT` answer `ENVIRONMENT?` with
   `false` (recognised-but-incomplete) rather than a flattering `true` — a
   deliberate override of the epic's own AC2, decided explicitly. Driven by
   `feedback_standards_compliance`.

3. **S9 silicon cadence held end-to-end.** Every delta story got a real-MicroBeast
   PASS before close. Emulators have hidden DIV-1, soft-EOF, and BIOS-MBB
   divergences before; the habit keeps paying.

4. **Mid-epic scope addition handled cleanly.** 23.6 wasn't in the plan; it came
   from a review finding, was itemised, project-lead-gated, and landed *before* the
   23.5 tag gate rather than after it.

## What didn't — challenges & growth areas

1. **Planning-doc §-citations wrong in 2 of 4 feature stories.** 23.2:
   `VALUE`/`TO` cited §6.1.2380 / §6.1.2295 — actually Core-Ext §6.2.2405 /
   §6.2.2295 (§6.1.2380 is `UNLOOP`). 23.4: `UD.` cited §8.6.1.1230 — that's
   `DNEGATE`; `UD.` isn't in DPANS94. Both caught at dev-time, but they should never
   have reached dev. The epic doc enumerated §-numbers from memory — exactly what
   `feedback_systematic_reference_check` exists to prevent.

2. **The `$8000` straddle halt is now a three-epic pattern — and Epic 22 predicted
   it.** Epic-22's AI-22-5 committed to pre-emptively migrating in-suite
   non-zero-bank probes to isolated fixtures *if Phase 5 grew the kernel*. That
   migration **did not happen** — kernel growth in 23.2 *and* 23.6 tripped the
   unguardable portal halt and was fixed reactively (de-colon the probe, drive it at
   interpret level) each time. The fix works, but it's whack-a-mole on a fragility
   named a full epic ago. **Follow-through: ❌ not pre-applied.**

3. **Verdict-grep harness papercuts recurred in nearly every story** — column-0
   anchoring (23.2/23.3/23.4), `\r`/CRLF stripping (23.4), NUL-byte `grep -a`
   (23.3). Each solved ad hoc, re-derived from scratch.

4. **Byte estimate low on cross-bank machinery — a fourth confirmation.** 23.2's
   bank-aware resolve/map/unmap trio drove +317 B against a ~120–215 B guess.
   `project_epic17_envelope` confirming itself: pure-addition estimates undershoot
   whenever cross-bank plumbing is involved.

## Key takeaways

1. **Load-bearing CR is the single highest-value habit in a polish epic** — the
   one story that mattered most for correctness (23.6) existed *only* because review
   refused to relax.
2. **A predicted fragility left un-pre-empted will recur** — AI-22-5 named the
   `$8000` straddle a full epic early; not acting on it cost reactive fixes in two
   more stories. Now greenlit (AI-23-1).
3. **Standards epics must cite the standard at planning time** — 2/4 stories
   carried wrong §-numbers from a memory-enumerated epic doc.
4. **Cross-bank byte estimates undershoot by ~2×** — fourth confirmation; budget
   cross-bank plumbing accordingly.
